# GES — Company and Industry Profile (shared context for all agents)

> Last reviewed: 2026-11-17. Items marked **VERIFY** should be confirmed with GES leadership
> or current public sources before they drive a recommendation.

## Company
- **GES (Global Experience Specialists)**, ges.com. Full-service partner for trade shows,
  exhibitions, conferences and corporate events.
- **Ownership:** Formerly the GES segment of Viad Corp. Viad agreed in 2024 to sell GES to
  private equity firm **Truelink Capital**; the sale closed around year-end 2024 (Viad then
  renamed itself Pursuit Attractions and Hospitality). **VERIFY** current ownership and portfolio.
- **Implication:** PE-backed. Expect a buy-and-build strategy: add-on acquisitions that widen
  capabilities, geography or margin, judged against the sponsor's hold period and exit story.

## What GES sells (VERIFY current business units)
- **Official services contractor / general services contractor** for show organizers:
  floor plans, carpet, furnishings, signage and graphics, rigging, electrical, cleaning.
- **Exhibitor services**: ordering, show-site labor, booth installation and dismantle.
- **Exhibit design and build** (custom and rental exhibits) for corporate brands.
- **Event production and audio-visual.**
- **Registration, badging, lead retrieval and event technology / data.**
- **Logistics**: freight, advance warehousing, material handling (drayage), fleet.
- **Geography**: primarily North America (strong in Las Vegas, Orlando and other major
  convention cities); historically UK/EMEA and a growing Middle East presence.

## Customers
- **Show organizers**: e.g. Informa, RX, Emerald, Clarion and trade associations. Multi-year
  contracts; renewals are a key revenue and risk driver.
- **Exhibitors / corporate brands**: buy booths, labor, graphics, AV, experiential.
- **Corporate event owners**: user conferences, sales kick-offs, product launches.

## Competitors
- **Freeman** (largest; private), **Shepard**, **Fern**, **Hargrove**, regional contractors;
  **Encore** and others in AV; many agencies and fabricators in exhibit design;
  specialist software firms in registration and event tech.

## Industry characteristics that matter for M&A
- **Revenue is lumpy**: show rotation (shows move cities), odd-/even-year shows, one-off
  mega-events. Normalize earnings over a full cycle.
- **Cyclical**: tied to corporate marketing budgets; COVID-19 nearly shut the industry in
  2020–21. Stress-test every case.
- **Labor is unionized and city-specific** (Teamsters, carpenters, IATSE, decorators), with
  work rules set by venue and local agreements.
- **Venue exclusives** (in-house AV, electrical, food) limit addressable markets in some cities.
- **Data and technology** (attendee data, lead retrieval, analytics) are growing, higher-margin
  and valued at higher multiples than labor-heavy services.
- **Sustainability** requirements (waste, reusable materials, carbon reporting) increasingly
  appear in organizer contracts.
- **Consolidation**: a few large contractors serve the biggest shows, so antitrust review is
  possible for contractor-on-contractor deals.

## Working strategic theses (to test, not assume)
1. Buy event-technology and data capabilities to raise margins and stickiness with organizers.
2. Add regional contractors or fabricators to fill city or capacity gaps.
3. Expand internationally where events are growing fastest (e.g. Saudi Arabia, UAE).
4. Buy experiential / creative capability to grow share of corporate brand budgets.
5. Consolidate logistics and warehousing for cost synergies.
